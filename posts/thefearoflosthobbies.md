<h1> 2023/2024 </h1>
<em>Now, gentlemen (and women), a short view back to the past...</em>
<p>It's March of 2023, lockdown finally ended for real, and we had to go back to high school full time.</p>
<p>There was only one problem though, I lost all fitness that I had from 7th/8th grade when I last went to track practice - and since all I did was sit in front of a computer and play games (or occasionally program) while talking to my friends; I've gained a lot of weight.</p>
<p>I started to be really unhappy to be in the position I was, and I felt like I should and can do better. My mom told me that I should go back to doing sports, but I dismissed her for a while since at that time I only liked rally racing; which I (we) obviously didn't have money for.</p>

<em>Side note: what a phase that was. Maybe I was trying to find something that I could say I can't do to avoid sports altogether? I really dislike cars and everything that comes with them now. (except the miata i guess)</em>

<p>Eventually I figured out I should go back to t&f, but if I went back immediately they would probably tell me to throw shot put or discus because of my weight. <strong>(NOT what I wanted to do)</strong></p>
<p>So, my mom signed me up to my local running club. At first I was against it since (at the time) I was 17/18 and most of the other members are 30+, but it turned out that they were really pleasant be around and run with. After the first few weeks, I really started enjoying running but didn't like their talks together (they were so much older that we didn't have much in common, not their fault ofc). I got my first Garmin watch and stayed with them for a couple more months but after that I went solo.</p>
<p>I love that watch. It allowed me to analyze the data after a workout (like the big nerd I am) and find what I had to work on in the future. I got my first adult MTB at or around that time, so I started combining the training days.</p>

<em>I didn't realize it at the time (even though the data was on the watch) that I was heavily overtraining. This led me to a few months of no progress until I eventually got sick and had to pause for 2-3 weeks.</em>
 
<p>After healing up - I went back to t&f, with a different team this time. While it was nice and fun, the sessions were way shorter than mine and I had spent quite a bit more time in public transit and stretching instead of running. I did this for a few more months until I eventually left (just simply not for me anymore).</p>

<h1>8th of May, 2024</h1>
<p>It was the last day of high school. Finally it was time to leave this hellhole. I had no real friends, and most of the class I was in wasn't even comprised of normal human beings - truly the lowest of the low.</p>

<em>The only good thing I can think of is the huge amounts of free time I had, not really something I can say about myself now.</em>

<p>The break still doesn't truly begin. I had to study for the entrance exam which was in July. I passed and trained properly and relentlessly until October. I cycled around 150km over 2 days in Italy, dropped my 5K to 21:10 and my 10K to 47min, increased my VO2Max from 48 to 51.</p>

<h1><strong>And then... med school started... </strong></h1>

<p>I cycled to med school aaaand... that's about it. I still keep dreaming about the day where I will truly get back to where I left off.</p>
<p>Fell in love, got friend-zoned, failed Anatomy 1 and had to repeat the year</p>

<p>As I write this, I still haven't passed Anatomy 1 (let alone 2) and I keep hearing from my friends that even when I eventually advance to the third semester, things are certainly NOT getting any easier.</p>

<p>Never have I wanted to be a pro athlete more than right now. But this is the path I picked, and if EVERYTHING goes even remotely to plan, I'll have a hell of a story to tell by the end of it.</p>

<h3>I still have my goals that will be very hard to achieve, but I wouldn't be me if I stopped trying.</h3>
