I've noticed some people have multiple accounts and since elementary school I've always wondered why.

I know nothing on the internet is ever private, so I just don't bother with having multiple accounts - my friends have my phone number and they are welcome to call me whenever.

## 1. Isn't it just too much noise?
I think that people hoping to separate parts of their lives or personalities just end up creating more work for themselves, and more noise. (and other dividing features like Instagram's "close friends" are making it worse)

Think about it this way - you create more work than you ever would have had with a single account.

**a.** posting photos - a handful of photos for the public/people-i-intend-to-keep-at-a-distance account *and* another two dozen more for the friends/close-friends-until-i-make-another-account-for-even-closer-friends account.
And if you mess up and post something that is meant for the different account or, God forbid, post something on your public story instead of your "close friends" story *on* your "close friends account" - people could see that as breaking character or realize they really aren't close with you at all.

**b.** answering messages - one type of people message your public account, the other ones message your private account - isn't that just more opportunities to mess up and send the wrong messages to the wrong people?

**c.** managing notifications - like here, comment there, of course half of those comments are just copy pasted from one account to the next or are incredibly generic. But if you turn those notifications off - what is the point then? If someone gives you constructive comments, suggestions or feedback you won't see it or respond within a reasonable amount of time (24/48h).

## 2. Dividing people
This is the worst part - especially if you are on the other side of this conversation. 

They are nice to you, talk to you every day, but you know there is a version of them you don't get to see. They probably don’t mean anything by it, but it can feel like you’re only getting the restricted version of someone.

(Here the question becomes what is even "real" and who is really pretending - but I guess that could be the question for another day.)


## That being said, I kind of understand why people do it.
It's the app that everyone uses (at least until the meta changes and people migrate).

Easier to do than a private blog or a diary? Although those alternatives remove the social element from social media so they are not directly comparable.

People generally like to know they are being loved and appreciated - the likes, notifications and comments are something that makes them feel good.

More control over who sees what? (Fair, but that control only goes so far. Anything you post can be screenshotted, and once it’s on a server, it’s out of your hands.)


## What I do
I think that if you are going to present yourself on the internet, you should present **YOU**

The blog is here to give me an opportunity to express my feelings about whatever I want in more depth than social media would allow me.
